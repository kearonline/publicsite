*The following is an excerpt from field notes produced during a visit to a safe sleeping lot in the City of San Diego, as part of the Urban Challenges in Homelessness course sequence. This visit involved conducting interviews with unhoused residents of the lot.*

At the start of our interview, where Quentin mentioned that he had just gotten hit by a car, had completely caught me off guard and left me wondering if I should continue with it knowing the circumstances. It also came right after an interview where the person decided to conclude the interview early after they realized they couldn’t continue, a likely result of our inquiries regarding their family that came with our typical lines of questioning. With the sensitivity of our topics still at top of mind, Quentin assured us that we were okay to continue, though the apprehension on our end remained.

Quentin also seemed to be a bit apprehensive about doing the interview, and being the one to lead the questioning, I was especially wary of touching upon topics that could have been triggering. However, between me and him, the rapport was built quickly as I began to chime in with some of my own experiences where I thought appropriate, being careful to not have my own voice dominate the conversation. By the end of the interview, both of us covered a lot of ground and the dynamic allowed the conversation to flow smoothly.

It was a reminder that, at least to me, the best approach to these kinds of interviews is to really just start with an open mind and allow the person themselves to guide changes in how I ask questions and move through different topics. As Quentin became more open with us, I also began to tailor my follow-ups and responses to become less structured, which in turn allowed for more tangential responses that led to deeper insights for us about what his experience is truly like.

While I still especially regret that our first interview brought to mind what appeared to be deep-rooted trauma for the person, both of this week’s interviews were examples of the inevitability of ethical dilemmas to arise when doing research that involves talking to real people about what they have been through. It proved to be a real learning experience.
