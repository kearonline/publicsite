My fellow Redmont citizens,

Almost exactly one year ago, I stood on the inaugural stage at the Government House in Hamilton to host the inaugural ceremonies for President Westray and Vice President Muffins29.

Never did I think then — or during the inaugural ceremonies I hosted for Presidents 218218Consumer or LilDigiVert — that I would eventually be the one delivering this speech on the dais today as the newly-inaugurated President of Redmont.

Only ten other players from a diverse range of backgrounds have held this position of extraordinary responsibility — End, West, Krix, HugeBob, Austin, Consumer, Digi, Ian, Deadwax, and Derpy. Today, I am honored and humbled to be joining this esteemed group in service to this great nation, and to begin my four months of work for the Redmont people.

By all means, I have had an unconventional career in public service prior to this point that began when I decided, on a whim, to apply for Media Advisor in the DPA under Secretary Vanquish, Ranger in the DER under Secretary Lone, and later, run for a Council position for the first time in Willow under Mayor Westray.

As much as I don’t like to cling to my past, I often look to this formative period of my life in Redmont to highlight the three most important lessons I learned early on, which I would like to pass forward today.

First, that teamwork and collaboration are crucial to be able to get shit done in our government. In a virtual democracy and government system that is as sprawling as this one, you absolutely cannot go it alone, both for your sake and for the sake of the institution you lead or serve in. 

I would like to thank my former colleagues in the DER, especially Lone, Sleepy, Mint, Venom, Drav, Birb, Zemitis, Toxic, and Iced, for helping to teach and demonstrate this important ideal for me during my time in the department. I still hold dear the several months I spent in that department, and it just goes to show how much that sense of culture and community can really improve someone’s experience in Redmont. Because that’s what happened to me.

Second, that everyone has something to contribute. This is something that has held true for not only myself, but for others throughout my time here, despite the many changes that have taken place elsewhere since I joined. Regardless of your playtime, what skills you possess, what positions you hold, or who you know, believe me when I say every voice, every opinion, and every concern matters.

I, too, was afraid of speaking out and voicing my concerns to the people in charge of dealing with them at the very start, but then I had a realization. None of that matters, especially here.

Take advantage of the fact that unlike in the real world, everyone here, except for a few, of course, is just one DM or ping away. Reach out when you feel that something isn’t right, when you see work that needs to be done and you believe you can do it, or if you have a suggestion to improve the lives of your fellow citizens.

I, myself, saw in Willow an opportunity to contribute to the town in an official manner and I decided to run for the position of Public Relations Advisor in July 2021 despite having no experience in government and little experience in the town itself. I ran then because I took it upon myself to help effect change and make a difference through public relations, and because I knew that my perspective as a newer player at the time mattered, just like all of yours do.

I know in my heart that there are probably many of you who are afraid to make the same type of leap I made because of the uncertainty you may feel about whether or not you’re qualified, you’ve been here long enough — all of that. Take these words as a sign that you should just go for it. Run for office. Apply for that government job. Start that business. Because there is no better time to make the leap than right here, right now.

Third, that it is important for those in positions of power to take chances on newer and more untested players, because I know from experience that it is a surefire way to ensure success for those who may come after. And I know that getting new players into positions where they can find their footing and serve the Redmont people with the crucial novel perspectives that they offer is not only important for representation, it is important for the continued survival of our democracy.

I am here today primarily because a president named Austin27 took a chance on nominating someone who had just been promoted to media manager and someone who had just gotten elected for the first time in Willow to the position of Secretary of Public Affairs. He took a chance on me, a player who was barely beginning to find his place on the server, and from that point on, I would seek to work hard to prove myself as a public servant. And without that opportunity, I don’t believe I would have ever 

I want to make sure that every single one of you — from the new players who are trying to figure out what you should do on here to the more experienced players who may feel that your time to make a difference has come and gone — deserves a realistic chance to serve the country you love in whatever way you see fit. 

Mark my words: our administration will seek to uplift a diverse range of voices throughout our term wherever; a couple of which I would like to shout now as first-time Cabinet members, including Commerce Secretary RandomIntruder, Interior Secretary QuazyOneAndOnly, and Press Advisor AdventurousAkio.

I will always keep in mind as I enter the Government House these three main lessons I learned early on, as well as everything that I have learned during my 18 months here.

Redmont is truly special as a place where you can be who you want to be without the potential consequences that exist in the real world. I urge you to use your time here to experiment, find your passions, or just have fun trying something new. I know I’ve learned so much not only about myself and my abilities through my time here, but also about much more than that; in many ways, I’ve found that Redmont truly is a microcosm of the real world and all of the dynamics that come with it.

But at the same time, don't let the elusive nature of DC’s escapism let you prioritize what you need to do here over what you need to do in the real world. I learned that the hard way. Nothing you do here can possibly be more important than staying on top of your class work, going out with your loved ones, or taking care of your mental health — or in fact, just taking care of yourself.

It may seem like common sense but behind every block character on this game — every username — is a real person — there is a real human, and no I don’t mean SomeHuman, on the other side of the screen whether or not you believe it. 

We must never lose sight of that and think clearly about what we seek to do wherever we decide to go on the attack — to call someone out — to rush to claim inactivity or incompetence, because we never know what’s going on on the other side.

I am deciding to bring this up in this somewhat sermon-like speech because I am optimistic that we as a nation are better than that — that together we can build a brighter future for Redmont that is more civil, more aware of our responsibility to acknowledge the in-game and real-world implications of our decisions, and more open, accountable, and accessible to everyone within its bounds.

To borrow some of my own words, almost 300 words in fact, these ideals are what will guide me and my administration, if elected, as we pursue an ambitious set of seven goals to move Redmont forward.

First, to make our government more open and accountable to the people — because we know that a government for the people cannot operate out of view of the people it is tasked with serving.

Second, to usher in a new era where collaboration is prioritized — where it is the norm once again, and not the exception — because the only way we can move forward as a collective is if we continue to work with each other, and not against each other.

Third, to build a nation that is more supportive of its newly-arrived citizens and its newly-formed businesses, who both take part in and play a critical role in ensuring that Redmont remains a land of opportunity for all.

Fourth, to improve education and accessibility nationwide in order to ensure our citizens get the resources they need to succeed, regardless of their level of experience or knowledge.

Fifth, to continue to support the development of our neighborhoods and our culture, because we know there remains much work to be done to improve  and maintain the unique character, aesthetics, and public spaces that exist within our communities.

Sixth, to keep our citizens and communities safe and healthy — whether that is through improving efforts to maintain public safety, developing ways for criminals to better co-exist with the DOJ in a roleplay context, preserving our wildlife and our uninhabited land, or improving our healthcare system.

And finally, to keep our government fiscally responsible, taking into account the many economic needs of our communities and businesses.

As we work towards these seven goals, and forgive me for this undercooked extended metaphor, this presidency will be like a four month car ride (even though I can’t drive).

I know we will hit a few speed bumps at high speeds, run into a few potholes that will slow us down, get some engine issues that’ll have us pull over, maybe rear-end another car, which I don’t know what that’s supposed to mean, but we will keep driving and pushing forward.

I know that what happens during the journey will be just as important as what is gained at the destination, when a new driver will take my place.

And while I will be in the driver’s seat, Sleepy in the passenger seat, and you, the people of Redmont in the back row, you will have considerable influence on the actions of me, the driver on this four-month road trip. You can ask us to pull over, make us slow down, or get us to crash — you will call the shots. 

Redmont, thank you for letting me be your driver for the next four months and for letting me use this dumb metaphor.

Thank you, Sleepy, for being by my side throughout this whole journey. Ever since I first met you in the DER on my first night as a ranger, from when we helped breed those horses, to when we helped defend the shelter during the Purge, to when we served as media advisors together in the DPA, we have done so much as a team. And I look forward to having you by my side as the next Vice President. 

Before I close this off, allow me to thank VerySmolBirb, who joined the server with me, and Muffins, West, Nicki, Ida, Taro, Vanilla, and so, so much more for being my support group — those who I turn to when things start to fall apart both here and in the real world. Thank you.  

All of us serving in government right now, including myself, are elected or appointed to serve the people of Redmont, and it is not only your right, but it is your duty to hold us accountable when we make mistakes.

Let me close by borrowing my own words again: I am certainly aware that I am not and never will be a perfect individual, citizen, or politician, as I know I’ve had my fair share of missteps in public service. But I know in my heart that I have always served and will always serve with your interests at the top of my mind.

Thank you again, Redmont, and I look forward to a productive and exciting next four months serving and working with you.

All aboard, Redmont.
