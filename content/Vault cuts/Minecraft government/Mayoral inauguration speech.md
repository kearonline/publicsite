Good afternoon.

I am extremely honored to be standing here today in the exact spot where just over ten months ago, Mayor Westray cut the ribbon to officially open the Town Hall behind me, marking the beginning of a new era for Willow.

Westray’s legacy won’t just be defined by the work he put into physically shaping Willow into what it is today by spearheading several expansions and major renovations, like this one, but also by how he helped bring our local government closer to you, the people that live here, work here, play here, and explore here.

As we gather here today to commence another era of prosperity for this storied town, we can all recognize that Westray’s story in Willow, defined by hard work, perseverance, and solidarity, is one that closely resembles the stories that lie within our community.

And that is why honoring that hard work that each and every one of you put into this town and ensuring that you have the opportunities you deserve and need to succeed and advance will be my number-one priority as your mayor. 

Because that’s what Willow has done. For as long as I can remember, this town has remained a bastion of democracy in the town sphere, allowing residents of all types, like me, the chance to offer their input on town matters, climb the ranks, and lead the way forward. It’s no surprise that other towns look to our democracy for inspiration.

That is why I plan on spearheading efforts to expand our current government. It is no longer enough to merely offer opportunities for residents to engage with town leadership — in a time when the levers of government are increasingly being held by the hands of a few, it is up to you, the people, to push back by getting involved.

You deserve a town government that represents you and your interests — one that prepares and empowers you, the next generation of leaders, for what lies ahead.

I know many of us are scared of that future. With our world facing so much uncertainty in the face of increasing political division and the transition to the unfamiliar, how can you not be?

But in the case of the Reveille transition, it does not have to be that way. 

Willow plans to commence our official transition to the new world on or before September 1, 2022, and in the period before then, I commit to making our development a team effort.

That is why I plan on taking an unprecedented amount of public feedback through polls, surveys, and more — not just to gauge opinions during the development process, but for matters that affect the day-to-day-lives of our residents.

That is why I plan on establishing a formal process of contracting citizens for our development and creating new Advisory Councils — councils composed of Willow residents that will serve to offer their input on different areas of consideration and assist our elected Council in making decisions for the town.

That is why I plan on making our entire development process transparent for the public through regular updates on our progress, giving credit where credit is due, and making public what needs to be public in terms of decisions made by our Council.

And in the spirit of transparency, let me begin by providing an update on what decisions have been already made so far in regards to the transition to address some of the burgeoning concerns of our residents:

First, most of Willow’s farm plots in Reveille will generally be smaller than they are in Hamilton now to allow more of our residents the chance to secure a farm plot and establish themselves in our town. While we have not yet decided on a process for transfers, we pledge to ensure that all current farm plot owners will be compensated fairly and generously during our transition to Reveille.

Second, our town hall and other government buildings will be expanded to match our expanding government and our growing number of town services.

Third, we will increase density and mixed-use development for our districts in Reveille, as the Willow Council recognizes that, frankly, it is not exactly easy to secure an affordable residential or commercial rental plot in Willow right now. Before the transition begins, we also aim to lessen the financial burden placed on our residents by way of decreasing rental prices and providing other avenues for relief.

These are only some of the plans we have for our move to Reveille so far, but before we try to make plans for our future, we must first address the issues that lie in the present while being proactive in our decisions to help avoid the mistakes we’ve made in the past.

The freedom of the press is sacred — our democracy cannot survive without the brave reporting of journalists across the nation on the issues that matter, casting light onto darkness. That is why I plan on taking steps to ensure that the journalism industry within Willow and outside of it remains strong, whether that be through signal-boosting, providing designated locations for newsstands, or expanding options for grants and loans provided by the Bank of Willow.

We plan to enact similar measures for the growing brewing industry that lies within our town, as we recognize the importance that this industry has for Willow. Public Relations Advisor DimpledRobin has proposed the creation of special plots and a district designated solely for our restaurant and brewing industries, which we aim to trial when we move to Reveille.

DimpledRobin has also played a critical role in our work to develop an exemption program for illicit substances in conjunction with our Sheriff and the Department of State. Later this month, with public input in mind, we plan to officially start that program, which will legalize the sale of certain illicit substances within our town limits.

Our Council, our next Deputy Mayor, which I hope to announce and appoint in the coming days, as well as our entire town government and constituency will be directly by my side as we work on addressing these challenges head-on.

We will do what needs to be done, find common ground amongst ourselves, and stand united in the pursuit of a better Willow. It is because of the community that we have developed here that I know that Willow’s best and brightest days are not behind us, but lie ahead of us. I’m ready and excited to work with all of you. Thank you.
