To tell you the truth, I have no idea what I’m doing with my life at the moment—and I’m perfectly fine with that, because at the end of the day, I am confident that no one truly knows deep down what they’re doing with theirs either. 

We are all connected through our inherent uncertainty in regards to ourselves, our lives, and the people we know and don’t know, living in uncertain times with uncertain dangers hidden around the corner at every step of the way. While sometimes they may seem genuinely insurmountable, we learn to endure these challenges to our stability as we begin to accept the fact that the only thing that will forever remain stable and permanent is our own impermanence on this planet. I have gone on to embrace that fact and have allowed it to guide my artistic practice in ways that go beyond the process.

That is why I feel most at ease when I break with convention and trends (within my own portfolio), or when I try to defy what might seem ‘possible’ in the visual space. Similar to how everything at a given moment is temporary, what is considered real or ‘inside the box’ is temporary as well, and is in constant flux. It is up to us as individuals to define the box, and I am determined to do my part—without filters on content or relevance. The time we all have here is limited, and if perfection isn’t achievable with any given, finite amount of time, I might as well embrace the imperfections, the little quirks that pop up in my work that arise from spontaneity, and what sets my work apart, for better or worse. Championing these mindsets has led me to be comfortable with just making what feels right to me and what matters to me, in whatever style, format, or medium.

Movement in all of its forms and how it impacts our lives is the single-most common thread that runs through my work, as the ways in which we progress and the speed in which we’re going through life through this ever-changing world are, too, ever-changing and uncertain. Sometimes, it may feel as if everything is slow, out of focus, blurry, and distorted while at other times, it may feel as if things are moving at a much faster pace than anticipated. Sometimes, I feel like I’m at a complete standstill, walking in a dark, undecorated hallway and moving through blank space while getting lost in the transition from one room to the other. Everyone and everything moves at different paces, though I know for certain that we are always moving and that nothing ever stays the same—that we are living in a state of permanent transience with no origin or destination.

No matter how chaotic the world can seem sometimes, I have always found comfort in the spontaneous experiences that I can share and enjoy with others—and that would mostly be through art, an activity I have always taken solace in to try and explain or work through everything and one where much of the creative process take place in spontaneity. I have found comfort in learning to appreciate the random events that happen in the everyday—the intermittent moments that put me on highs and make me forget about the lows, the reminders of timelessness for certain objects and places, and the beauty that arises in the conflicts between the natural world and mankind. I have learned to appreciate the little details within the environments in which I reside: San Diego, where I am attending university, and Los Angeles, where I was raised—both places I will forever appreciate for the diverse perspectives and cultures they exposed me to as I was and currently am growing up.

I try my best to explore the relationship between these joys and anxieties in my work—not just to share them and leave them out in the open for anyone to synthesize or to interpret, but mainly to pass on or offer a reminder that to endure and be affected by this uncertainty in not knowing what your place in the world is means that you are human, even if you take in the chaos of the modern world in different ways as your counterparts. 

To best exemplify these differences, I treat all of my works as individual pieces that exist in their own little worlds and have their own unique flairs as a result of the parts of the process of art-making being conducted independently of those for pieces from before. While there are truly no universal elements that I apply to every piece, there are some that I often turn to to convey specific feelings or emotions. Jumbled, cut and pasted, stretched, and distorted text and images in surrealist collage form, for instance, are common elements in my work as they help me visualize disorder (both in good ways and bad ways) and spontaneity of ideas.

It is my greatest hope that these messages of doubt and hope alike come through strongly in my work, as these repeating ideas and certain uncertainties ground me and keep me going as an artist with one goal in mind: to help myself and others come to terms with our reality.
