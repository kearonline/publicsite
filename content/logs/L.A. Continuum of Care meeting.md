Today, I attended the [L.A. Continuum of Care (CoC) meeting](https://www.lahsa.org/documents?id=10304-la-coc-general-membership-special-meeting-agenda-10-9-2026) that was abruptly adjourned a few minutes after it began. Why? There were not enough seats in the Zoom meeting to accommodate every member who wanted to vote, which exceeded a thousand people. Under the CoC's bylaws, everyone who resides in its service area, spanning the entire county with the exception of the cities of Glendale, Pasadena, and Long Beach, which have their own, is considered a member of the CoC and is able to vote on matters before the board. The L.A. CoC was set to vote on the board's recommendation to select the County as the entity responsible for fulfilling all four of its primary responsibilities as the CoC's collaborative applicant, homeless management information system (HMIS) manager, Coordinated Entry System (CES) manager, and Point-In-Time (PIT) Count administrator. 

Questions that [lingered after the meeting](https://mayor.lacity.gov/news/mayor-bass-city-councilmembers-issue-statement-la-continuum-care-board-meeting-breakdown-due) including "why couldn't they just upgrade the Zoom?" spoke to the awkward dynamic that can ensue when policies intended to maintain a basic level of access for all can come into conflict with the  realities of hosting public meetings, especially when there's a need to pivot. 

Quoted below is an excerpt from a message I wrote to one of my professors shortly after the meeting was adjourned that captures my immediate reaction and how it connected to a lot of my research from earlier this year:
  
>I’m outside the LAHSA Building after having just left what was supposed to be the CoC member meeting. It’s quite unfortunate that it was postponed: the shock among attendees in that room was palpable and unlike anything I’ve seen in other public meetings that I’ve attended. 

>What has happened here has restored my interest in looking at some of the barriers to engagement for members of the public attending open meetings. I wrote about the topic for my undergraduate thesis and I’m interested in writing a brief report about this one as a further case study for when policy intended to maintain a basic level of access (e.g. the Brown Act) can result in further barriers for those who do attend when these events take place and hosts are perhaps not fully prepared to handle surges in attendance nor able to pivot in a timely manner. This also takes into consideration the times during which meetings are held (during working hours for most) and the base level of commitment needed to attend. ...

>... I’ve learned a lot about the LA CoC these past several days and I fully intend to attend the rescheduled meeting if I am available.
