# City Propositions

> [!danger]- Prop TE, ULA tax exemption: NO <code class="red">Endorsed</code>
>Prop TE appears to have good intentions at first glance: grant temporary reprieve from the Measure ULA "mansion tax" (transfer tax) to homeowners in the Pacific Palisades affected by the devastating wildfires that ravaged the community last year. Palisadeans deserve relief, but a better approach is necessary to ensure funding streams for affordable housing development from Measure ULA funding remain stable to keep projects on track in the rest of the City.
>
>Prop TE's passage would weaken Measure ULA's provisions significantly by reducing revenue by millions per year, acting as a significant diversion of wealth from top earners away from affordable housing development as was initially intended. I still believe that Measure ULA is in desperate need of reform to allow new multi-family development to break even, as many have sounded the alarm about its unintended consequences since its passage in 2022. A significant reduction in revenue as proposed by Prop TE would pose a serious challenge to future attempts at reform that would further take away from that much-needed pool. 

> [!success]- Cht Amd LA, Infrastructure/budget: YES <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
> Cht Amd LA, recommended by the Charter Reform Commission, would put the City on a two-year budget cycle and make the City's finances more resistant to the political whims of the day. It includes common sense streamlining reforms to contracting and the City's business enterprises. This measure would be good for the health of the City's finances.

> [!success]- Cht Amd PL, Planning authority: YES <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
> Cht Amd PL, recommended by the Charter Reform Commission, would make it so planning commission appeals on project decisions go to a citywide body, eliminating the possibility of disparate outcomes across different areas of the City. It includes further reforms that'd reduce unnecessary friction in the planning and permitting process. It's much needed.

> [!caution]- Cht Amd EE, Ethics and elections: NO <code class="orange">Recommended</code>
> Cht Amd EE is a genuine mixed bag. While its provisions around penalties for campaign finance violations and greater authority for police and fire department independent investigators are good, its reduction of required City Council meetings to only one per week instead of three, as well as its increase to the required number of signatures, would outweigh many of the positive benefits of this measure. I don't believe this measure is good for public accountability.

> [!success]- Cht Amd PRK, Parks budget: YES <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
> Cht Amd PRK would ensure parks, long underfunded in the City while the police budget continues to balloon, get an adequate proportion of the budget for its regular allocation. It's a no-brainer.

> [!hint]- Cht Amd PRT, Port budget: YES <code class="blue">Recommended</code>
> Cht Amd PRT comprises a series of straightforward reforms to the Harbor Department and the Airport Commission, some of which would mandate the use of funds for waterfront access projects.

> [!hint]- Cht Amd SC, LAUSD campaign rules: YES <code class="blue">Recommended</code>
> Cht Amd SC is pretty simple, extending existing campaign finance rules to Board of Education elections. It just makes sense.

> [!caution]- Measure FD, Fire Dept sales tax: NO <code class="orange">Recommended</code>
> Measure FD is a complicated one. I think the Fire Department needs to be funded, and that it needs more funding: the handling of the Palisades Fire clearly demonstrates this. I also think that existing limitations on increases to property taxes and assessments have made it damn near impossible to fund basic City services to adequate levels without having to resort to seeking approval on the ballot for new sales taxes. But in a time when Angelenos are finding it more difficult to even get by, and in a region with one of the highest sales tax burdens in the entire country, I'm not sure if an additional 0.5% is the way to go. That's not to say I'm concerned about where this revenue would go, as it's written in a way that's pretty airtight and ensures that funds only go to the Fire Department. I'm mostly concerned about the precedent that this sets. Will other departments look to regressive sales taxes for a more stable revenue stream for City services? The status quo around taxes is seriously broken. I'll be voting against this measure in protest of that alone, though it's likely to pass.

# County Propositions

> [!caution]- Measure A, Public union disputes: NO <code class="orange">Recommended</code>
> I'll defer to the [LA Forward voter guide](https://laforwardguide.org/guide/la-county-ballot-measures), which much more eloquently explains why you should vote no on this measure. It would, through the imposition of "impartial binding arbitration" for disputes between the County and public sector unions including those of the sheriff's deputies, actually reduce public accountability.

> [!success]- Measure E, Ethics commission: YES <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
> Measure E would make the County's ethics commission more independent through its affirmation of governance reform task force recommendations. Perhaps more importantly, it would fix [the disastrous error](https://laforwardguide.org/guide/la-county-ballot-measures) made during the passage of 2024's Measure G, which accidentally repealed the community investment and anti-carceral provisions of Measure J. It's another no-brainer.

# State Propositions

> [!hint]- Prop 1, Housing bonds: YES <code class="blue">Recommended</code>
> Prop 1 bonds would go towards housing affordability programs. I mean, yeah.

> [!hint]- Prop 2, Rainy day fund: YES <code class="blue">Recommended</code>
> Prop 2 increases the amount the state holds in its reserves to make sure essential services continue to be funded during economic downturns. That's good.

> [!success]- Prop 3, High-income tax: YES <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
> Prop 3 keeps the tax on the top 2% of earners that's been in place since 2012. This funding stream is critical for K-12 and community college systems and would otherwise expire in 2031 if this doesn't pass.

> [!success]- Prop 4, Public fundraising: YES <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
> Prop 4 doesn't actually establish public fundraising programs for campaigns. It just eliminates the state ban on it with some necessary guardrails that prevent the use of funds allocated for essential services.
> 
> Public fundraising programs are good for democracy and grassroots campaigns. In cities like ours that do have it, these often take the form of "matching funds", which allows for less-established candidates to remain competitive with others backed by well-off private donors by having funds donated by City residents amplified by the City.

> [!success]- Prop 5, Recall reform: YES <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
