# Introduction

Angelenos this election have a major opportunity to change the direction of our City: two of three at-large elected offices will be on the ballot along with two City Council seats with no incumbent.

Each of these runoffs conveniently follow a dynamic resembling that of many banner races across the country, where voters will choose between "more of the same" or "something new," between more of the malaise and inaction that has defined much of our municipal politics or a new focus on the issues that we're experiencing on the ground and [to get around](https://www.instagram.com/reels/DcOys-6uGRB/).

As a proxy war takes hold between the establishment and a new brand of progressivism that has found success by embodying a kind of *sewer socialism* or *pothole politics* (that some have tried and [failed to imitate](https://www.instagram.com/reels/DcAC8oiRFc8/)), our local races will be the battleground that'll determine whether the L.A. area will follow the likes of cities from New York City, to Providence, to Seattle. 

While the alignment of each of our City candidates on that spectrum could not be more clear and present real choices with real consequences, many of the elections above the County level do not. It's a shame that quite a few were decided during the primary, with some of our best shots at turning things around coming just [oh-so-painfully short](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/governor/). The mixed-bag results of our state's primary have left much of the remaining elections as choices between Bland & Vaguely Progressive Normiedem and An Actual Republican or different variations of that.

Nonetheless, there are some genuinely difficult choices to make this year, many of whom are in the local and state ballot propositions, covered in [the second part](https://whoiskear.com/2026-los-angeles-voter-guide/the-measures-on-the-ballot) of this guide. I hope that this guide with the research I've done as a mostly-normal guy will help you to make yours or put you on the direction to make the choices that align best with your beliefs. 

Happy voting!

# Methodology

## Priorities

I'm rooting for candidates who:
* **Actively resist the undue influence** of the political establishment and corporations in their actions, in office or otherwise
* **Propose forward-looking policy with substance** instead of leaning on listless resistance
* **Promise bold actions that recognize the urgency** of this moment to address many of the converging crises we're facing

In office, they should be willing to champion:
* **Humane, Housing First solutions to homelessness** that treat the unhoused with dignity
* **YIMBY approaches to building** and eliminating barriers to building housing of all kinds, everywhere, to address affordability
* **Accountable and transparent governance**, especially concerning the expenditure of public funds, benefits, and partnerships
* **Reforms to reduce undue regulatory burdens** facing development, small business, and anyone looking to do business with government

## Assumptions

I make a ton of assumptions about you, the reader:
1. **You are on the 'progressive' side of the political spectrum.** At minimum, you want to see your government do things for the benefit of the common good. But you also believe the political establishment and how much incumbency and tradition and the 'natural order of things' (in a political context) are valued often gets in the way of candidates who seek to make lasting progress.
2. **You were not considering voting for a Republican anyway.** I did not find it to be useful to make recommendations in races that fell along party lines between a Democrat and Republican solely on the basis of candidates being a Democrat. 

## Interests

I'm an avid follower of L.A. local politics who's somewhere on the political left, well enough to call myself a leftist. My academic background is in urban planning, housing and homelessness, and municipal governance.

This election cycle, I've served as a volunteer Field Lead and Design Creative Lead for Nithya Raman's campaign for Mayor of Los Angeles. I've also volunteered for DSA-LA, CA YIMBY, and Marissa Roy's campaign for City Attorney.

## Tags 

I distinguish between different levels of recommendation:
* <code class="green">Endorsed</code> I unequivocally believe this candidate is the best option for this seat, and would volunteer my time or money to get them elected.
* <code class="blue">Recommended</code> I would absolutely vote for this candidate, but I have some reservations about them, their viability, or their edge over the other option.
* <code class="orange">No Viable Alternative</code> I would still vote for this candidate purely as a form of harm reduction, as there is no truly 'good' option available for this seat in this election.
* <code class="gray">Safe Seat</code> This non-endorsed candidate is likely to win this election.
* <code class="gray">*</code> This candidate is an incumbent.

# City of Los Angeles

> [!success]- Mayor: Nithya Raman <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
> **Karen Bass has been an MIA mayor.** She's failed to meet the moment to address some of the worst crises the City has faced in modern times. She's failed to act meaningfully to get the unhoused into permanent housing. Under her leadership, the City has failed to meet its most basic responsibilities to its residents. For these reasons, and many more, Mayor Bass does not deserve a second term.
> 
> **Nithya Raman is the clear-eyed leader Los Angeles needs.** Her proposals are realistic and pragmatic, and focus on wonky policy improvements, many at the department level, that would ensure that the City, well, *works*. In her time in office, she's championed renter protections that have kept rents lower for millions and a services-not-sweeps approach to the unhoused in her district leading to significant reductions in unsheltered homelessness. When much of the City continues to struggle with affordability and homelessness, it's important to look to who's been responsible for the progress that has been made. Look no further.
> 
> Councilmember Raman's understated boldness in taking on the establishment from her first (and not the last) time defeating a sitting incumbent to her leadership in proposing much-needed reforms to the City Charter, including a proposal to expand the Council that would have weakened her own power, have not made her many friends in City Hall. But while the suffocating nature of these institutions have turned some occasionally-promising, would-be reformers away from their original mission, she has kept her focus on these critical issues and taken accountability when she's made mistakes: much of it's been to her detriment, but the people of the City are all the better for it.
> 
> This year, she has run a principled, lowkey campaign with a positive vision for Los Angeles' future that has rankled much of the City's political establishment, and for good reason. A Mayor Raman would upend the City Hall status quo of inaction and show Angelenos what's possible from their leadership when they're willing to be accountable to the people and make decisions based on the best available information. How this campaign has turned out thus far has made it abundantly clear that people are starting to realize that we deserve better. We deserve a mayor that's actually willing to do the work to change the institutions that cause harm, instead of deferring to them.
> 
> **Vote Nithya Raman for Mayor of Los Angeles.**

> [!success]- City Attorney: Marissa Roy <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
> **John McKinney would continue the "legacy" of his defeated predecessor.** Backed by police unions and pro-business groups, he's also endorsed by everyone who's as far to the right as the deep blue L.A. electorate permits.
> 
> **Marissa Roy is the people's choice.** Her proposals are likely to transform the quiet row office into a powerful vehicle for advancing the public interest, akin to Kenneth Mejia's approach to the City Controller's office. She would use the powers of this office to its full potential to defend renters, protect immigrants, and take on big business: to represent the City against anyone who seeks to take advantage of the vulnerable.
> 
> Her endorsements in this race by groups across the liberal-to-leftist spectrum ranging from the County Democratic Party, to DSA-LA, to the Working Families Party, not to mention most of the City's labor unions, demonstrate a rare level of unity around a candidate. It's well earned.
> 
> **Vote Marissa Roy for City Attorney of Los Angeles.** 

## City Council

> [!success]- Councilmember, Dist. 3: Barri Worth Girvan <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
> Barri Worth Girvan, an ally of Valley progressive powerhouse and reformist champion Supervisor Lindsey Horvath, is the clear choice for this district. While she would fit squarely in the mainstream of the progressive bloc in the City Council, a la Katy Yaroslavsky one district down, she is a good fit and our best shot at getting a progressive elected in this moderately left-leaning district.
> 
> **Vote Barri Worth Girvan for City Council.**

> [!success]- Councilmember, Dist. 9: Estuardo Mazariegos <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
> Estuardo Mazariegos, a longtime community organizer with ACCE Action and decades-long history championing grassroots movements throughout South Central, would give this district the thoughtful, on-the-ground representation that it deserves and has lacked with embattled incumbent Curren Price at the mantle. The contrast in options in this election is striking: a candidate who rejects corporate donations and the mainstream L.A. political-labor establishment and another who's propped up by those powerful interests. I know who I'd choose.
> 
> **Vote Estuardo Mazariegos for City Council.**
