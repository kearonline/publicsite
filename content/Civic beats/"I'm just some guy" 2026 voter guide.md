# Introduction

Angelenos this election have a major opportunity to change the direction of our City: two of three at-large elected offices will be on the ballot along with two City Council seats with no incumbent.

Each of these runoffs conveniently follow a dynamic resembling that of many banner races across the country, where voters will choose between "more of the same" or "something new," between more of the malaise and inaction that has defined much of our municipal politics or a new focus on the issues that we're experiencing on the ground and [to get around](https://www.instagram.com/reels/DcOys-6uGRB/).

As a proxy war takes hold between the establishment and a new brand of progressivism that has found success by embodying a kind of *sewer socialism* or *pothole politics* (that some have tried and [failed to imitate](https://www.instagram.com/reels/DcAC8oiRFc8/)), our local races will be the battleground that'll determine whether the L.A. area will follow the likes of cities from New York City, to Providence, to Seattle. 

While the alignment of each of our City candidates on that spectrum could not be more clear and present real choices with real consequences, many of the elections above the County level do not. It's a shame that quite a few were decided during the primary, with some of our best shots at turning things around coming just [oh-so-painfully short](https://calmatters.org/california-voter-guide-2026/governor/). The mixed-bag results of our state's primary have left much of the remaining elections as choices between Bland & Vaguely Progressive Normiedem and An Actual Republican or different variations of that.

Nonetheless, there are some genuinely difficult choices to make this year, many of whom are in the local and state ballot propositions, covered in [the second part](https://whoiskear.com/2026-los-angeles-voter-guide/the-measures-on-the-ballot) of this guide. I hope that this guide with the research I've done as a mostly-normal guy will help you to make yours or put you on the direction to make the choices that align best with your beliefs. 

Happy voting!

# Methodology

## Priorities

I'm rooting for candidates who:
* **Actively resist the undue influence** of the political establishment and corporations in their actions, in office or otherwise
* **Propose forward-looking policy with substance** instead of leaning on listless resistance
* **Promise bold actions that recognize the urgency** of this moment to address many of the converging crises we're facing

In office, they should be willing to champion:
* **Humane, Housing First solutions to homelessness** that treat the unhoused with dignity
* **YIMBY approaches to building** and eliminating barriers to building housing of all kinds, everywhere, to address affordability
* **Accountable and transparent governance**, especially concerning the expenditure of public funds, benefits, and partnerships
* **Reforms to reduce undue regulatory burdens** facing development, small business, and anyone looking to do business with government

## Assumptions

I make a ton of assumptions about you, the reader:
1. **You are on the 'progressive' side of the political spectrum.** At minimum, you want to see your government do things for the benefit of the common good. But you also believe the political establishment and how much incumbency and tradition and the 'natural order of things' (in a political context) are valued often gets in the way of candidates who seek to make lasting progress.
2. **You were not considering voting for a Republican anyway.** I did not find it to be useful to make recommendations in races that fell along party lines between a Democrat and Republican solely on the basis of candidates being a Democrat. 

## Interests

I'm an avid follower of L.A. local politics who's somewhere on the political left, well enough to call myself a leftist. My academic background is in urban planning, housing and homelessness, and municipal governance.

This election cycle, I've served as a volunteer Field Lead and Design Creative Lead for Nithya Raman's campaign for Mayor of Los Angeles. I've also volunteered for DSA-LA, CA YIMBY, and Marissa Roy's campaign for City Attorney.

## Tags 

I distinguish between different levels of recommendation for candidates:
* <code class="green">Endorsed</code> I unequivocally believe this candidate is the best option for this seat, and would volunteer my time or money to get them elected.
* <code class="blue">Recommended</code> I would absolutely vote for this candidate, but I have some reservations about them, their viability, or their edge over the other option.
* <code class="orange">No Viable Alternative</code> I would still vote for this candidate purely as a form of harm reduction, as there is no truly 'good' option available for this seat in this election.
* <code class="gray">Safe Seat</code> This non-endorsed candidate is likely to win this election.
* <code class="gray">*</code> This candidate is an incumbent.

I used the following levels of recommendation for measures:
* <code class="green">Endorsed YES</code>/<code class="red">Endorsed NO</code> I unequivocally endorse this vote.
* <code class="blue">Recommended YES</code>/<code class="orange">Recommended NO</code> I would recommend this vote, but I have some reservations.
* <code class="yellow">No Recommendation</code> I don't feel comfortable making a recommendation given that there are very valid arguments for or against this proposition.

# The people on the ballot

## City of Los Angeles

> [!success]- Mayor: Nithya Raman <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
> **Karen Bass has been an MIA mayor.** She's failed to meet the moment to address some of the worst crises the City has faced in modern times. She's failed to act meaningfully to get the unhoused into permanent housing. Under her leadership, the City has failed to meet its most basic responsibilities to its residents. For these reasons, and many more, Mayor Bass does not deserve a second term.
> 
> **Nithya Raman is the clear-eyed leader Los Angeles needs.** Her proposals are realistic and pragmatic, and focus on wonky policy improvements, many at the department level, that would ensure that the City, well, *works*. In her time in office, she's championed renter protections that have kept rents lower for millions and a services-not-sweeps approach to the unhoused in her district leading to significant reductions in unsheltered homelessness. When much of the City continues to struggle with affordability and homelessness, it's important to look to who's been responsible for the progress that has been made. Look no further.
> 
> Councilmember Raman's understated boldness in taking on the establishment from her first (and not the last) time defeating a sitting incumbent to her leadership in proposing much-needed reforms to the City Charter, including a proposal to expand the Council that would have weakened her own power, have not made her many friends in City Hall. But while the suffocating nature of these institutions have turned some occasionally-promising, would-be reformers away from their original mission, she has kept her focus on these critical issues and taken accountability when she's made mistakes: much of it's been to her detriment, but the people of the City are all the better for it.
> 
> This year, she has run a principled, lowkey campaign with a positive vision for Los Angeles' future that has rankled much of the City's political establishment, and for good reason. A Mayor Raman would upend the City Hall status quo of inaction and show Angelenos what's possible from their leadership when they're willing to be accountable to the people and make decisions based on the best available information. How this campaign has turned out thus far has made it abundantly clear that people are starting to realize that we deserve better. We deserve a mayor that's actually willing to do the work to change the institutions that cause harm, instead of deferring to them.
> 
> **Vote Nithya Raman for Mayor of Los Angeles.**

> [!success]- City Attorney: Marissa Roy <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
> **John McKinney would continue the "legacy" of his defeated predecessor.** Backed by police unions and pro-business groups, he's also endorsed by everyone who's as far to the right as the deep blue L.A. electorate permits.
> 
> **Marissa Roy is the people's choice.** Her proposals are likely to transform the quiet row office into a powerful vehicle for advancing the public interest, akin to Kenneth Mejia's approach to the City Controller's office. She would use the powers of this office to its full potential to defend renters, protect immigrants, and take on big business: to represent the City against anyone who seeks to take advantage of the vulnerable.
> 
> Her endorsements in this race by groups across the liberal-to-leftist spectrum ranging from the County Democratic Party, to DSA-LA, to the Working Families Party, not to mention most of the City's labor unions, demonstrate a rare level of unity around a candidate. It's well earned.
> 
> **Vote Marissa Roy for City Attorney of Los Angeles.**

# The measures on the ballot

## City of Los Angeles

> [!danger]- Prop TE, ULA tax exemption: NO <code class="red">Endorsed</code>
>Prop TE appears to have good intentions at first glance: grant temporary reprieve from the Measure ULA "mansion tax" (transfer tax) to homeowners in the Pacific Palisades affected by the devastating wildfires that ravaged the community last year. Palisadeans deserve relief, but a better approach is necessary to ensure funding streams for affordable housing development from Measure ULA funding remain stable to keep projects on track in the rest of the City.
>
>Prop TE's passage would weaken Measure ULA's provisions significantly by reducing revenue by millions per year, acting as a significant diversion of wealth from top earners away from affordable housing development as was initially intended. I still believe that Measure ULA is in desperate need of reform to allow new multi-family development to break even, as many have sounded the alarm about its unintended consequences since its passage in 2022. A significant reduction in revenue as proposed by Prop TE would pose a serious challenge to future attempts at reform that would further take away from that much-needed pool. 

> [!success]- Cht Amd LA, Infrastructure/budget: YES <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
> Cht Amd LA, recommended by the Charter Reform Commission, would put the City on a two-year budget cycle and make the City's finances more resistant to the political whims of the day. It includes common sense streamlining reforms to contracting and the City's business enterprises. This measure would be good for the health of the City's finances.

> [!success]- Cht Amd PL, Planning authority: YES <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
> Cht Amd PL, recommended by the Charter Reform Commission, would make it so planning commission appeals on project decisions go to a citywide body, eliminating the possibility of disparate outcomes across different areas of the City. It includes further reforms that'd reduce unnecessary friction in the planning and permitting process. It's much needed.

> [!caution]- Cht Amd EE, Ethics and elections: NO <code class="orange">Recommended</code>
> Cht Amd EE is a genuine mixed bag. While its provisions around penalties for campaign finance violations and greater authority for police and fire department independent investigators are good, its reduction of required City Council meetings to only one per week instead of three, as well as its increase to the required number of signatures, would outweigh many of the positive benefits of this measure. I don't believe this measure is good for public accountability.

> [!success]- Cht Amd PRK, Parks budget: YES <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
> Cht Amd PRK would ensure parks, long underfunded in the City while the police budget continues to balloon, get an adequate proportion of the budget for its regular allocation. It's a no-brainer.

> [!hint]- Cht Amd PRT, Port budget: YES <code class="blue">Recommended</code>
> Cht Amd PRT comprises a series of straightforward reforms to the Harbor Department and the Airport Commission, some of which would mandate the use of funds for waterfront access projects.

> [!hint]- Cht Amd SC, LAUSD campaign rules: YES <code class="blue">Recommended</code>
> Cht Amd SC is pretty simple, extending existing campaign finance rules to Board of Education elections. It just makes sense.

> [!caution]- Measure FD, Fire Dept sales tax: NO <code class="orange">Recommended</code>
> Measure FD is a complicated one. I think the Fire Department needs to be funded, and that it needs more funding: the handling of the Palisades Fire clearly demonstrates this. I also think that existing limitations on increases to property taxes and assessments have made it damn near impossible to fund basic City services to adequate levels without having to resort to seeking approval on the ballot for new sales taxes. But in a time when Angelenos are finding it more difficult to even get by, and in a region with one of the highest sales tax burdens in the entire country, I'm not sure if an additional 0.5% is the way to go. That's not to say I'm concerned about where this revenue would go, as it's written in a way that's pretty airtight and ensures that funds only go to the Fire Department. I'm mostly concerned about the precedent that this sets. Will other departments look to regressive sales taxes for a more stable revenue stream for City services? The status quo around taxes is seriously broken. I'll be voting against this measure in protest of that alone, though it's likely to pass.

## County of Los Angeles

> [!caution]- Measure A, Public union disputes: NO <code class="orange">Recommended</code>
> I'll defer to the [LA Forward voter guide](https://laforwardguide.org/guide/la-county-ballot-measures), which much more eloquently explains why you should vote no on this measure. It would, through the imposition of "impartial binding arbitration" for disputes between the County and public sector unions including those of the sheriff's deputies, actually reduce public accountability.

> [!success]- Measure E, Ethics commission: YES <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
> Measure E would make the County's ethics commission more independent through its affirmation of governance reform task force recommendations. Perhaps more importantly, it would fix [the disastrous error](https://laforwardguide.org/guide/la-county-ballot-measures) made during the passage of 2024's Measure G, which accidentally repealed the community investment and anti-carceral provisions of Measure J. It's another no-brainer.

## State of California

> [!hint]- Prop 1, Housing bonds: YES <code class="blue">Recommended</code>
> Prop 1 bonds would go towards housing affordability programs. I mean, yeah.

> [!hint]- Prop 2, Rainy day fund: YES <code class="blue">Recommended</code>
> Prop 2 increases the amount the state holds in its reserves to make sure essential services continue to be funded during economic downturns. That's good.

> [!success]- Prop 3, High-income tax: YES <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
> Prop 3 keeps the tax on the top 2% of earners that's been in place since 2012. This funding stream is critical for K-12 and community college systems and would otherwise expire in 2031 if this doesn't pass.

> [!success]- Prop 4, Public fundraising: YES <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
> Prop 4 doesn't actually establish public fundraising programs for campaigns. It just eliminates the state ban on it with some necessary guardrails that prevent the use of funds allocated for essential services.
> 
> Public fundraising programs are good for democracy and grassroots campaigns. In cities like ours that do have it, these often take the form of "matching funds", which allows for less-established candidates to remain competitive with others backed by well-off private donors by having funds donated by City residents amplified by the City. Campaign finance, as we all know, is broken. This is a much-needed tool that would help counter the impact of big money in elections.

> [!success]- Prop 5, Recall reform: YES <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
> Prop 5 would make it so after an elected official is recalled, vacancies are then filled by succession or special election instead of in a second replacement question. This is a pro-democracy reform that ensures that replacement candidates aren't elected by plurality in a low-turnout election where anti-recall voters would be averse to picking an alternative.

> [!caution]- Prop 37, Homebuyers loan: NO <code class="orange">Recommended</code>
> Prop 37 is also complicated. It's well established that homeownership is one of the primary pathways to building equity under our current system, and that more people should be able to enter this market. Where this proposition concerns me is its exclusivity to new-build residences, and specifically single-family homes. That scope of eligibility is too narrow and provides an undue advantage to single-family developers.

> [!question]- Prop 38, Research bond <code class="yellow">No Recommendation</code>
> I'll defer to [this article](https://calmatters.org/politics/elections/2026/10/prop-38-california/) by CalMatters on why there are questions about this proposition's bonds mostly going towards a single research institute.

> [!danger]- Prop 39, Voter ID: NO <code class="red">Endorsed</code>
> Prop 39 would lead to voter disenfranchisement by imposing an ID requirement that's both redundant and inequitable. This is an easy choice.

> [!success]- Prop 40, Billionaire tax: YES <code class="green">Endorsed</code>
> Prop 40 would be a significant, though drop-in-the-bucket (for the billionaires) redistribution of wealth from the top (less than) 1% of earners to the rest of us. It's a bold measure that would create a massive pool of funds for health care in a time when it's much needed.

> [!danger]- Prop 41, Tax audits: NO <code class="red">Endorsed</code>
> Prop 41 is written to nullify Prop 40 if it passes with more votes. Vote no.

> [!danger]- Prop 42, Property taxes: NO <code class="red">Endorsed</code>
> Prop 42 is written to nullify Prop 40 if it passes with more votes. Vote no.

> [!danger]- Prop 43, Tax threshold: NO <code class="red">Endorsed</code> 
> Prop 43 is perhaps one of the most dangerous, anti-democratic measures on the ballot this cycle, increasing and rendering virtually impossible any and all future efforts at the local level to raise revenue via special taxes. It does so by increasing the voter threshold to pass a special tax measure to two-thirds instead of a simple majority. If this passes, cities and municipalities across California will struggle to fund services and capital expenditure projects without the option of adding additional revenue streams via voter approved taxes. Approval by a simple majority should be considered sufficient. Vote no.

> [!danger]- Prop 44, Clinic funding: NO <code class="red">Endorsed</code>
> Prop 44 is intended to increase accountability for community health clinics by having them spend 90% of their revenue on services. But it's a one-sized-fits-all restriction that would lead to some services that don't count under the measure's definition of services being massively cut. Let community health clinics decide themselves what should be funded for their patients' well-being and health and vote no.

> [!question]- Prop 45, Environmental review <code class="yellow">No Recommendation</code>
> Prop 45 intends to reform the CEQA process for environmental review by limiting the length of time permitted to collect public input, a necessary step towards eliminating some of the pathways for the weaponization of CEQA by NIMBYs against any new housing development. In that case, this measure is good. Others argue, rightfully so, that this goes too far so as to effectively deal a death blow to the CEQA regime of environmental protections in allowing these reforms to extend to infrastructure projects that may cause great harm to surrounding communities. CA YIMBY has [a fantastic writeup](https://cayimby.org/blog/proposition-45-2026/) on the many arguments for and against Prop 45, and I encourage you to weigh all of these arguments to make a vote here that aligns best with your values.
